# UX Laws Review

Ten psychology/HCI principles, popularised by Jon Yablonski's *Laws of UX* (lawsofux.com), turned into practical design checks.

## The laws

1. **Jakob's Law** - People spend most of their time on other products and expect yours to behave the same way. Use familiar conventions (logo links home, magnifier means search, cart top-right). Be original in your core value, not your navigation.
2. **Fitts's Law** - Bigger, closer targets are faster to hit. Make primary actions large and easy to reach; touch targets at least 44x44px.
3. **Hick's Law** - More choices means slower decisions. Cut options per step, suggest a default, hide secondary options behind progressive disclosure.
4. **Miller's Law** - Working memory holds roughly 7 (+/-2) items. Chunk information into small meaningful groups.
5. **Postel's Law** - Accept messy input generously, output clean and consistent results. Use forgiving validation and specific, helpful error messages.
6. **Peak-End Rule** - Experiences are remembered by their best/worst moment and their ending. Design a strong high point and a satisfying finish.
7. **Aesthetic-Usability Effect** - Attractive interfaces feel easier to use and earn forgiveness for small flaws. Polish matters.
8. **Von Restorff Effect** - The item that stands out gets noticed and remembered. Highlight the one thing that matters most, sparingly.
9. **Tesler's Law** - Every task has irreducible complexity; the system should absorb it (smart defaults, automation), not the user.
10. **Doherty Threshold** - Responses under ~400ms keep users engaged. Use instant feedback, skeleton screens and optimistic UI.

## How to apply

When reviewing or designing a screen or flow, check it against all 10 in order. For each issue found:
- name the law,
- point to the specific element,
- give a concrete fix.

End with the top 3 highest-impact changes. When asked about a single law, explain it with one good and one bad real-world example.
